SYSPRO
Full End-to-End Optimization & Flow Architecture
Business Operating System for SMEs
Confidential — Internal Architecture Document
Executive Summary
Syspro is designed as a multi-tenant ERP for SMEs, built on Next.js, NeonDB (PostgreSQL), and deployed on Vercel. This document defines the full optimized flow architecture — covering all modules, their inter-connections, RBAC governance, automation chains, and recommended new modules to make Syspro a true business operating system for Nigerian and African SMEs.
The core optimization principle is: every action is a trigger, not a dead end. Right now each module operates as an island. This document describes how to connect them into six automated chains that eliminate manual handoffs and make the system self-running for routine operations.
The Fundamental Problem
Modules are disconnected — a won deal does not create an invoice, a hired employee does not set up payroll, low inventory does not generate a PO
The Finance dashboard has hardcoded ₦0 values — no live data
Reports use setTimeout simulation instead of real API calls
The expense submitter identity is hardcoded as "current-user" — breaking audit integrity
There is no RBAC — every user sees everything regardless of role
No automated workflow routing — approvals require manual intervention at every step

2. CRM → Finance Chain
The most important automated chain in the system. When a deal is won in CRM, three things should happen simultaneously and automatically — no human needed to kick off the next step.

Chain Steps
Step 1 — Deal Won (CRM Trigger)
Contact progresses through Lead → Opportunity → Deal stages
When status changes to "won", an internal event is published immediately
Three parallel automated actions fire at the same time
Step 2 — Three Parallel Auto-Actions
Finance: Auto: Draft invoice
Line items pre-populated from deal scope and pricing
Payment terms pulled from customer profile
Linked PO attached if one exists on the deal
Projects: Auto: Create project
Milestones created from deal terms and delivery schedule
PM assigned based on deal owner or round-robin rule
Team notified via in-app notification
Inventory: Auto: Pick order
Items from deal scope reserved in inventory
Warehouse team notified for fulfillment

Step 3 — Downstream Auto-Actions
Finance manager reviews and sends invoice — customer receives it automatically
Payment received → invoice auto-reconciled against Finance Account balance
Project milestone completed → progress invoice triggered automatically
Items shipped → COGS posted to GL instantly, no manual journal entry needed
All events → Reports refresh automatically via the event bus
3. HR → Finance Lifecycle
HR is the most underconnected module for SMEs. Every hire, payroll run, and departure has direct financial consequences that currently require manual entry. The optimized flow makes every HR event a Finance trigger.
Figure 3: HR lifecycle — every employment event auto-triggers Finance, GL, RBAC, and asset management
Onboarding Auto-Chain
When an employee is hired, four things should happen automatically without any Finance team involvement:
Payroll setup — employee auto-added to the monthly payroll cycle with correct salary band, PAYE bracket, and pension contribution
GL account mapping — employee cost center assigned and mapped to the correct department GL account
RBAC role assignment — system access permissions granted based on role (Staff, Manager, etc.) and branch scope
Asset assignment — laptop, tools, and equipment logged against the employee record with depreciation tracking
Monthly Payroll Run
PAYE, pension, and net pay calculated per employee automatically
Finance payment queued for bank transfer on run approval
GL journal entry posted — salary expense recorded against cost center
Budget actuals updated — headcount cost compared against forecast
Expense Claims Flow
Staff expense claims connect HR and Finance. The optimized flow: Staff submits → auto-routed to correct approver by RBAC → approved → reimbursement queued as either addition to payroll or direct bank transfer → GL posted → budget actuals updated.
4. RBAC & Approvals Engine
RBAC is prerequisite number one before any automation is meaningful. Every automated trigger needs to know who to route to and what they are allowed to see. Without RBAC, automations fire to the wrong people or expose sensitive data incorrectly.
Figure 4: RBAC approval routing matrix with threshold-based escalation path
Role Definitions
Staff / Employee
Submits expenses, timesheets, and leave requests only
Sees only their own submissions and approval status
No access to invoices, bills, payments, reports, or other employees' data
Scoped to their own branch — cannot see other branches
Department Manager
First-level approver for team expenses up to ₦50,000
Approves leave requests for their team
Sees only their department's budget and actuals — no cross-department visibility
Submits their own expenses which escalate up to Finance Manager
Finance Manager
Mid-level approver for expenses and bills between ₦50,000 and ₦500,000
Reviews, sends, and manages the invoice lifecycle
Views all financial reports but cannot edit Chart of Accounts
Manages PO approvals and vendor bill matching
Finance Admin
Processes payment runs — vendor payments and reimbursements
Manages Chart of Accounts and GL accounts
Performs period close and exports financial reports
Sets budget limits and approval thresholds system-wide
Cannot submit expenses — separation of duties
Tenant Owner
Full override access on all modules
Creates and deactivates users, assigns roles
Executive-level approver for transactions above ₦500,000
Sees consolidated reports across all regions and branches
Auditor (Read-Only)
Views all modules, all history, all transactions — no exceptions
Cannot create, edit, or approve anything
Sees full audit logs including expense_audit_logs
Can export all reports — ideal for external auditors or compliance reviews
Access is time-limited per engagement
Threshold-Based Escalation
The auto-escalation path ensures every transaction reaches the correct approver without manual routing:
Under ₦50,000 — Department Manager only
₦50,000 to ₦500,000 — Department Manager → Finance Manager
Above ₦500,000 — Department Manager → Finance Manager → Finance Admin → Tenant Owner
Branch Scoping
All roles are scoped to regionId and branchId. A Finance Manager in the Lagos branch cannot see Abuja branch data. A Department Manager can only approve expenses tagged to their branch. Tenant Owner and Auditor are the only roles with cross-branch visibility. This is enforced at the API route level via withRole() middleware, not just the UI.
What Needs to Be Built
roles table — role definitions per tenant with configurable thresholds
user_roles table — maps users to roles with regionId and branchId scope
permissions table — granular action permissions per role (can_approve_expenses, can_view_reports, etc.)
useRole() hook — client-side role context for UI gating (show/hide sidebar items, action buttons)
withRole() middleware — server-side API route protection, returns 403 for unauthorized actions
Approval routing engine — reads user_roles to determine the correct queue for each transaction
5. Procurement → Vendor → Payment Loop
Procurement is the spend control gateway. Every outgoing payment — vendor invoices, service bills, equipment purchases — should pass through this loop. The 3-way match is the key automation that eliminates manual bill reconciliation.
Figure 5: Procurement loop with budget gate, PO creation, and 3-way match auto-approval
Flow Steps
Step 1 — Purchase Request
Staff or Project Manager identifies a spending need
Request submitted with description, amount estimate, GL account, and budget category
System immediately checks available budget allocation — auto-block if insufficient
Step 2 — Budget Gate
If budget available — PO creation proceeds automatically
If budget insufficient — request flagged, manager notified, requires override approval
Override can be granted by Finance Manager or Tenant Owner depending on amount
Step 3 — PO Creation & Vendor Notification
PO auto-created with all line items, terms, and delivery expectations
Vendor notified automatically by email via the existing email.ts service
PO linked to the originating purchase request for full audit trail
Step 4 — Receipt Logging
When goods or services are delivered, receipt is logged in the system
Procurement Officer confirms receipt against PO line items
Any discrepancies flagged immediately before vendor invoice arrives
Step 5 — 3-Way Match
System compares three documents automatically: PO + receipt confirmation + vendor invoice
If all three match within tolerance — Bill is auto-approved, no manual review needed
If mismatch detected — sent to Finance Manager for manual review with discrepancy highlighted
Matched bill → queued for next payment run automatically
6. Inventory → Finance Automation
Every inventory movement has a financial consequence. The optimized flow ensures that stock sold, received, or running low all trigger the correct Finance entries automatically — no warehouse team should ever need to post a journal entry.
Figure 6: Inventory automation — stock movements trigger COGS, balance sheet updates, and reorder POs
Three Inventory Triggers
Stock Sold
Triggered when a CRM deal is completed or a pick order is fulfilled
COGS entry posted to GL automatically against the correct expense account
Revenue recognition linked to the related invoice
Inventory asset value on the balance sheet updated immediately
Stock Received
Triggered when a vendor PO delivery is confirmed in Procurement
Inventory asset value increases on the balance sheet
Linked to the originating vendor bill for cost matching
Location and batch tracking updated if warehouse management is enabled
Stock Low — Reorder Trigger
When stock level drops below the configured reorder threshold
Auto-draft PO created and sent to Procurement for review
Preferred vendor and last-purchase price pre-populated on the PO
Procurement Officer reviews and approves — no Finance team involvement needed
Inventory Valuation Engine
Supports FIFO, LIFO, and weighted average cost methods
Balance sheet inventory line item updates in real time as stock moves
COGS on the P&L updates with every sale — no month-end manual adjustment needed
Variance between purchase price and standard cost auto-posted to a variance GL account
Recommended: Warehouse Management Module
To fully automate inventory, a lightweight warehouse management module should be added with: stock locations (bin/shelf/zone), batch and expiry date tracking, serial number tracking for high-value assets, and multi-location transfers with automatic GL entries.
7. Event-Driven Reports
Currently the Reports page uses setTimeout simulation instead of real API calls, and the Finance dashboard has hardcoded ₦0 values. The fix is an event-driven architecture where every financial action across the system publishes to an internal event bus, and reports recalculate automatically.
Figure 7: Event-driven report refresh — replaces all setTimeout simulation and hardcoded dashboard values
How It Works
Every status change across all modules — deal won, invoice paid, expense approved, payroll run, PO matched — writes a row to a finance_events table in NeonDB
A database trigger or background job reads the event and updates a cached summary view
The dashboard reads from the cached summary — near-zero query time, no expensive aggregations on every page load
Full report endpoints (P&L, balance sheet, cash flow, aged receivables) recalculate on demand against the live transaction tables — no simulation
Reports to Build / Fix
P&L — Revenue vs cost by period, department, and branch
Cash flow statement — Operating, investing, and financing activities
Balance sheet — Assets, liabilities, and equity at any point in time
Aged debtors — Overdue invoices by customer and days overdue
Aged creditors — Overdue bills by vendor and days overdue
Vendor spend analysis — Spend by vendor, category, and period
Budget variance — Actuals vs budget by department and GL account
Payroll summary — Headcount cost by department and period
Dashboard Fixes Required
Wire all metric cards to the NeonDB cached summary view
Recent transactions list — fetch from API, not empty array
Expense list — replace TODO comment with actual server-side fetch on load
All currency displays — confirm ₦ (Naira) is applied consistently
Date ranges — allow filtering by period (this month, last month, YTD, custom)

The Six Automated Chains — Summary
Once fully implemented, Syspro runs on six automated chains that cover the entire business operating cycle:
Chain 1 — Revenue: Contact → Lead → Opportunity → Deal won → Draft invoice → Sent → Payment → GL reconciliation → P&L update. Zero manual handoff after deal close.
Chain 2 — Spend control: Staff submits expense → RBAC routes to correct approver → Approved → Bill generated → Budget actuals updated → Payment queued → GL posted → Variance report refreshed.
Chain 3 — Procurement: Need identified → Budget checked → PO raised → Vendor notified → Delivery logged → 3-way match → Bill auto-approved → Payment run → Vendor analytics updated.
Chain 4 — Payroll: Employee hired → Payroll setup → Monthly run → GL posted → Bank transfer → Budget headcount actuals updated.
Chain 5 — Inventory: Item sold → COGS posted → Stock level updated → Reorder threshold hit → Auto-PO drafted → Balance sheet updated.
Chain 6 — Reporting: Every event in all five chains above writes to finance_events table → cached summary view recalculates → dashboard reads from cache → all hardcoded values become live.
